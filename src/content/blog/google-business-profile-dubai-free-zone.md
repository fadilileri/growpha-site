A free zone company in Dubai can have a real office, real staff, and a valid trade license, and still get its Google Business Profile suspended for looking fake. The reason has nothing to do with the business being illegitimate. It has to do with Google's verification system being built around a storefront model that most free zone setups don't fit.

## Why verification goes wrong first

Free zones like DMCC, JAFZA, and Dubai South typically house dozens or hundreds of companies in the same tower, often the same floor, sharing a reception desk and a business centre address. Google's automated verification checks look for signals of a distinct, independently operating location: a unique suite number, visible signage, consistent address formatting across the web. A shared business centre address trips several of those checks at once, and Google's default response is to flag the listing rather than approve it. Businesses that go in expecting a straightforward verification, the way a retail shop would experience it, are usually the ones surprised by a rejection or a suspension a few weeks after approval.

## The category problem specific to free zone services

Many free zone companies operate in consulting, trading, media production, or professional services that don't map onto Google's business categories the way "restaurant" or "dental clinic" does. Picking a broad category because it feels like the safe general option, "business consultant" for a company that specifically does trademark registration and IP consulting, for example, weakens local relevance instead of protecting it. Google's local ranking algorithm rewards specificity: a narrower, more accurate category matches more search intent directly and gives Google more confidence that the listing is real and correctly self-described.

## Building presence signal without a public storefront

Because a public street-level presence usually isn't part of a free zone setup, the profile needs to work harder to establish legitimacy through the fields Google can verify directly: consistent business name, address, and phone number across the website, license documentation, and any directory listings; a service area configuration rather than pretending to be a walk-in location; and photos that show the real working environment, meeting rooms, team, signage inside the office, rather than stock imagery or an empty logo.

## A practical optimisation checklist

1. Select the most specific matching category available, then add secondary categories only if they genuinely describe services offered, not aspirational ones.
2. Set up the profile as a service-area business if clients don't visit the office, which removes the exact address from public display while keeping the business eligible for local search.
3. Upload photos taken at the actual premises: the office interior, team members, any physical signage in the building, dated and geotagged where possible.
4. Build a steady review generation cadence, a handful of genuine reviews collected consistently over months, rather than a burst of twenty reviews in one week that reads as manufactured to Google's spam systems.

Appeals matter too when verification gets rejected the first time around. A rejection letter rarely explains the specific trigger, so the reapplication should proactively address the likely cause: attach the trade license, add a photo of the exact suite door or floor directory entry showing the business name, and request a live video verification call rather than waiting for another automated review that is likely to fail the same way.

## How this feeds into the wider local pack ranking picture

Everything above exists to solve one problem: proving to Google that the free zone business is a genuine, independently operating entity worth showing to nearby searchers. Once verification and category selection are solid, the profile competes on the same proximity, relevance, and prominence factors as any other Dubai business, reviews, citation consistency, and engagement with the profile itself. The businesses that struggle long-term in the local pack usually aren't the ones with weak GBP optimisation. They're the ones still fighting an unresolved verification or category mismatch that undermines everything built on top of it.
