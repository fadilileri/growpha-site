Most businesses that ask for a Wikipedia page assume the bar is having a real company with real customers and a real website. Wikipedia's bar is higher and far more specific than that, and a large share of the businesses asking don't clear it yet, whatever their revenue or headcount looks like.

## What "notable" means on Wikipedia

Wikipedia's notability guideline for organisations requires "significant coverage in reliable secondary sources that are independent of the subject." Every word in that phrase is doing real work. Significant means the coverage has to go beyond a passing mention; a company needs to be the actual subject of the piece, not one line in a roundup article. Reliable means an established publication with editorial standards, not a blog, a directory listing, or a pay-to-play "top 10 companies" listicle. Independent is the strictest filter of all: the source can't be the company's own website, a press release the company issued, or an article that's clearly rewritten from that press release with no original reporting added.

## What counts, and what doesn't

A genuine feature article in a recognised business or trade publication, one where a journalist independently researched the company, interviewed people, and reached their own conclusions about its significance, counts. A profile in an industry magazine covering how the company built something specific and why it mattered to the sector counts too, provided the publication has an editorial process separate from advertising.

A press release counts for almost nothing, even when it gets picked up and republished by dozens of outlets, because none of those republications involved independent reporting. A "50 companies to watch" listicle rarely counts either. Most of these lists are pay-for-inclusion or built from submitted company bios rather than independent editorial judgment, and Wikipedia's editors who review new articles for notability recognise the pattern immediately. A single quote from a company spokesperson buried inside a broader industry trend piece doesn't count, because the company isn't the subject of that coverage, just a source cited within it.

## The honest reality for most businesses

A genuinely useful, profitable, well-regarded company can still fail this test completely if the media coverage it has is all product mentions, sponsored placements, or self-published content. This isn't a flaw in the guideline. It exists specifically to keep Wikipedia from becoming a directory of every business that wants a page there, and the practical result is that a large share of companies asking for a Wikipedia presence simply don't have the independent coverage yet to support one, regardless of how established or successful they are.

For a business in that position, the honest options are limited: wait until genuine independent coverage accumulates naturally, invest in earning that kind of coverage through real PR work rather than paid placements, or accept that a Wikipedia page isn't the right project right now and put the same budget into a channel where the return doesn't depend on clearing someone else's editorial bar first. None of those three options is a shortcut around the guideline itself, and any agency offering to "guarantee" a Wikipedia page regardless of existing coverage is either planning to write around the rules or setting a client up for an article that gets deleted soon after it publishes.

## The assessment before any engagement

Before agreeing to take on a Wikipedia project, the first step is a straightforward source audit: pulling every piece of existing media coverage on the business and sorting it into three piles, coverage that's clearly independent and substantial, coverage that's borderline, and coverage that won't count at all under Wikipedia's guidelines. If the first pile is thin, the honest answer is that the business isn't ready yet, and the right recommendation is building coverage first rather than attempting a page that Wikipedia's own volunteer editors will likely flag for deletion within days of it going live.
